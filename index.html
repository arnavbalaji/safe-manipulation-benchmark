OopsieVerse: A Safety Benchmark with Damage-Aware Simulation for Robot Manipulation

Paper-ID 1018

Abstract

While robotic manipulation capabilities have advanced rapidly, physical safety remains a major barrier to deploying household robots: task success is insufficient if the robot damages itself or its surroundings. Simulation offers a harm-free alternative to costly and dangerous real-world training and evaluation, yet existing simulators lack general mechanisms to detect, quantify, and represent damage. To address this gap, we introduce OOPSIEVERSE, a unified simulation framework and benchmark for damage-aware household manipulation. At a theoretical level, OOPSIEVERSE augments an existing Markov Decision Problem with additional damage-related observations, rewards and/or termination conditions based on user preferences. OOPSIEVERSE provides damage as an explicit, physically-grounded, and task-agnostic signal by converting sources such as contact forces, temperature changes, and liquid interactions into corresponding mechanical, thermal or fluid damage. OOPSIEVERSE comprises two core elements: (1) DAMAGESIM, a simulator-agnostic framework for detecting and quantifying damage during navigation and manipulation, and (2) a suite of household tasks designed to evaluate common damage modes and distinguish between task completion and safe execution. We demonstrate the generality of our framework by instantiating DAMAGESIM in two simulators with different physics backends, OmniGibson (Nvidia Omniverse) and RoboCasa (MuJoCo). We further showcase the utility of OOPSIEVERSE across multiple use cases, including (1) guiding safer demonstration collection via real- time damage feedback, (2) learning safer manipulation policies through damage-conditioned imitation learning and reinforcement learning, (3) benchmarking the safety of state-of-the-art Vision Language Action policies, and (4) improving real-world safety of sim-to-real transferred policies. Together, our results highlight the potential of OOPSIEVERSE as an open-source foundation for systematic, scalable research on safe robot manipulation.

DamageSim is our simulator-agnostic damage-aware instrumentation layer that makes physical safety measurable during household navigation and manipulation by tracking an object-centric "health" state and updating it online from common simulator signals (e.g., contact forces, temperatures, and liquid exposure). Concretely, it converts these interaction signals into mechanical, thermal, and fluid damage, exposing damage as a physically grounded, task-agnostic signal that can be used as observations, rewards, and/or termination conditions—without changing the underlying task dynamics. We demonstrate portability by instantiating DamageSim in OmniGibson/Omniverse and RoboCasa/MuJoCo, enabling consistent safety evaluation and learning across simulators.

OopsieBench is a suite of 32 household manipulation tasks designed specifically to expose the gap between task completion and safe execution. Each task includes realistic "tempting shortcuts" that can achieve the goal while causing damage, alongside safer strategies that require more careful interaction—enabling systematic measurement of safety–performance trade-offs using DamageSim's health signals. The benchmark spans diverse scenes, objects, horizons, and damage modalities, and is cross-platform (tasks in both OmniGibson and RoboCasa), with human demonstration data included for learning-based baselines and safety-aware imitation learning.

How can OopsieVerse enable safer policy learning and evaluation?

Safer demonstration collection + imitation learning: We add real-time DamageSim health feedback to teleoperation, showing it yields safer demonstrations, and we further use the damage signal to curate/weight data so imitation policies improve safe success with minimal loss in task completion across five tasks.

Damage-aware reinforcement learning: We use DamageSim-derived damage penalties as a universal safety cost for RL—steering and fine-tuning existing policies and training from scratch—demonstrating substantially higher safe completion compared to task-reward-only baselines.

VLA evaluations: We benchmark a state-of-the-art VLA model (NVIDIA GR00T) and find a consistent gap where high task completion often masks damaging interactions, causing much lower safe success and degraded average environment health—highlighting why damage-aware benchmarks are necessary.

Sim-to-real transfer: Policies trained with damage-aware signals (e.g., health-filtered IL) transfer to a real Franka Panda setup and qualitatively reduce unsafe outcomes (e.g., spills/unsafe contacts) compared to damage-unaware baselines over repeated trials.
